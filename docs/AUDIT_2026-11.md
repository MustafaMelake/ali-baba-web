## 1. Executive summary

**Overall risk: moderate.** The core is solid:
- `placeOrder` cannot be made to bill a client-supplied price.
- Discounts come from one engine, read against one `now`.
- Every mutation and every admin page re-checks the session and role.
- MANAGER branch scoping holds on every order read and write I traced.
- The order chain's `Restrict` policies are intact.
- Every raw SQL statement is parameterised.

I found **no cross-branch data leak, no privilege escalation, and no way to bill the wrong amount.**

The problems are at the edges:
- **Pricing consistency:**
  - Ordinary product edits can make the storefront show a product at **0 EGP**, which `placeOrder` then refuses (C-1).
  - Checkout can bill a different total from the one the customer was shown, with no warning (H-3).
- **Abuse and scale:**
  - There is **no rate limiting anywhere**. Both public writes (`placeOrder` and `recordVisitEvents`) can be scripted without limit (H-1, H-4).
  - The visitor-funnel query grows with traffic and sits inside the staff dashboard's only data call. At your expected traffic it will make `/admin` slow and then unusable, and anyone can trigger that on purpose (H-2).
- **Data safety:** after the R2 migration, one image object can be shared by several rows. Deleting one row then permanently deletes another row's only copy of the image (M-1).

### Traffic-readiness verdict: **ready for a soft launch, not for a traffic spike**

- **Soft launch (normal word-of-mouth traffic): yes.** The catalog really is ISR-cached (§A.5). Money is exact. Orders are safe.
- **A spike, a paid campaign, or a hostile actor: not yet.** Three things fail under load or abuse:
  1. The staff dashboard (H-2). Its cost grows with visits, and anyone can inflate it.
  2. Fake cash-on-delivery orders (H-1). The per-phone throttle is beaten by rotating numbers and by parallel requests.
  3. `/shop` (M-4). It is the one uncached storefront page, and with price filters on it prices the whole catalogue on every hit.

Fixing the **Top 5** in §3 is roughly 1–2 days of work. After that the verdict becomes "yes".

### 🔴 CRITICAL

#### C-1: Product edits can put a 0 EGP price on the storefront (the free-product guard can be bypassed) — ✅ RESOLVED (see Resolution log)
**Verified.** *(DEA HIGH-1, upgraded because your rubric says a card showing a price `placeOrder` won't bill is Critical.)*

**Files:**
- `src/app/admin/products/actions.ts`:
  - `updateProduct`: line 91 changes `categoryId`; lines 105–112 update or create variant prices.
  - `createProduct`: lines 240–266.
- The guards that do exist:
  - `fixedAmountProblem` (`src/lib/actions/promotions.ts:173-199`), which runs only on promotion create, update, and toggle.
  - `freeProductProblem` (`src/lib/actions/collections.ts:183`), which runs only on collection writes.

**Invariant broken:** "No promotion may make a product free". The guard covers only one side: the promotion write. The catalogue write is unguarded.

**Scenario** (a normal admin workflow, no attacker needed):
1. A live "Eid −30 EGP" FIXED_AMOUNT promotion targets the *Oriental Sweets* category. The cheapest item there is 45 EGP, so the promotion passed its guard.
2. An admin adds a new product, "Basbousa piece", at 25 EGP to that category. Adding a cheaper "Mini" size to a promoted product, or lowering a variant's price, has the same effect.
3. `resolvePrice` floors the discounted price at 0. Every surface that prices through the engine shows **0.00 EGP** with a "−100%" badge: the category card, PDP, search, wishlist, homepage hero, and cart.
4. The customer adds it to the cart. At submit, `placeOrder` (`orders.ts:262-269`) refuses with "can't be ordered right now" and rolls back the **whole order**, including the customer's other items.

**Blast radius:**
- **No money is lost**, because `placeOrder` refuses the line.
- But real products are publicly advertised as free (an obvious screenshot risk), and every cart containing one cannot check out until the customer works out which line to remove.
- It lasts until someone notices.

**Smallest fix:**
- Extract `fixedAmountProblem`'s logic into a shared helper that takes a list of `(variantId, newPrice, productId, categoryId)`.
- In `createProduct` and `updateProduct`, run it inside the write plan, after computing the final variants and category and before committing.
- Refuse when any live **or scheduled** FIXED_AMOUNT promotion reaching a changed variant has `value >= newPrice`. "Reaching" means through the variant, the product, the *new* category, or any collection the product is in.
- Do M-2 in the same PR.

### 🟠 HIGH

#### H-1: Fake cash-on-delivery orders are effectively unlimited, and the per-phone throttle is also a race
**Verified.**

**Files:**
- `src/lib/actions/orders.ts:103-115`: the throttle.
- `orders.ts:96-102`: the comment justifying it.
- `src/lib/validators.ts:301,318-332`: the phone rule.

**Assumption broken:** the throttle's docblock says that under a burst "the ceiling still holds within one request of the limit". That is false.
- The `COUNT` runs **outside** the transaction, before any insert.
- N parallel requests with the same phone all read `pendingCount = 0`, and all N create orders.

**Scenarios:**
- **Same phone, in parallel.** A script fires 50 concurrent `placeOrder` calls with `customerPhone: "01012345678"`. All 50 pass the throttle, and 50 PENDING orders land in one branch's queue.
- **Rotating phones.** `normalizeEgyptMobile` accepts any `1[0125]\d{8}`, which is about 400 million valid numbers. A script picks a random valid number for each order.
  - There is no IP limit, no CAPTCHA, and no account requirement (guest checkout is a deliberate design choice).
  - It can place orders as fast as the function scales.
- **Example attacker.** A competitor floods the PENDING queue with plausible orders. Staff either waste time phoning fake numbers or, worse, prepare and dispatch them.

**Blast radius:**
- The staff queue for every branch, plus wasted food and delivery trips. This is the classic Egyptian COD fraud pattern.
- Each fake order also runs a full order transaction against Neon.

**What is correct:** phone normalisation itself. Every spelling it accepts (010…, +2010…, 002010…, 2010…, separators, Arabic-Indic and Eastern Arabic-Indic digits) collapses to one canonical number. I found **no spelling that slips past the throttle**. Unusual spellings are *refused*, not let through (see L-4).

**Smallest fix:**
1. **Per-IP rate limit at the edge.** See H-4. A Vercel WAF rule needs no code.
2. **Make the throttle atomic.** Inside the transaction, run `SELECT pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext(${phone}))` and then the `count`. Concurrent orders for one phone then serialise, and the cap becomes exact.
3. **Correct the false comment.**

#### H-2: The visitor funnel grows with traffic and takes the staff dashboard down with it, and anyone can force that
**Verified.** *(SG H1, upgraded: I traced the coupling to the page and the public write that feeds it.)*

**Files:**
- `src/lib/actions/dashboard.ts:252-255`: `visitEvent.findMany` with no `take`, then in-memory Sets in `src/lib/visitor-funnel.ts:105-113`.
- `src/app/admin/page.tsx:78-92`: the funnel shares **one `Promise.all`** with `getDashboardStats`. Any error renders "Your manager account isn't linked to a branch", for admins too.
- `src/lib/actions/visits.ts:40-58`: the public write.
- `src/lib/validators.ts:850-878`: the batch schema and bot filter.
- No pruning job exists anywhere in `src/` or `scripts/`.

**Assumption broken:** the loader's own docblock says it is "fine for tens of thousands of events a month".

**Traffic maths at the expected 6,600 visits/day:**
- The tracker sends roughly one call per page view and records views, product views, and add-to-carts.
- Estimate about 4–5 events per visit, so **≈ 26–33k rows/day**.
- The 30-day window then holds **≈ 0.8–1.0 M rows** within a month of launch, and passes 100k rows in the first week.
- Every load of `/admin` pulls the whole window: three columns of roughly 60–80 bytes each.
  - Data transfer: **≈ 60–80 MB from Neon per page load.**
  - Server memory: **hundreds of MB** of JavaScript objects, plus up to four Sets of unique visitor ids.
- Expected effect: multi-second, then tens-of-seconds loads; then function timeouts or out-of-memory errors.
- Neon egress cost grows with the number of dashboard loads.
- Because of the shared `Promise.all`, the **revenue cards and recent orders fail with it.** That is the page staff open first.

**Write side:**
- At 26–33k rows/day, about 1 insert every 3 seconds, Postgres and the Neon pooler are nowhere near their limits. Even a 50k-visitors-per-hour spike is about 60 inserts/second.
- The real write-side costs are:
  - about **0.6–1 M function invocations/month** for the tracker alone;
  - a Neon compute that **never scales to zero**.

**Abuse:**
- `recordVisitEvents` is a public Server Action. Its id is in the client bundle.
- The bot filter is just a User-Agent regex, so sending a browser User-Agent defeats it.
- Each call inserts up to 20 rows. One script at 50 requests/second inserts **3.6 M rows/hour**.
- The next dashboard load then tries to pull millions of rows. **The dashboard is down until rows are deleted by hand.**

**Smallest fix, cheapest first:**
1. **Decouple the funnel:** give it its own `try/catch` (and ideally `<Suspense>`), so a funnel failure cannot blank the stats. Also stop showing the "not linked to a branch" message for every error. About 15 lines.
2. **Move the counting into SQL:** `COUNT(DISTINCT "visitorId")` grouped by type and Cairo day, using the same `AT TIME ZONE ${STORE_TZ}` pattern as `analytics.ts`. The page then reads about 60 rows instead of a million.
3. **Bound the table:** add a daily Vercel Cron that deletes rows older than 90 days, in batches of `createdAt < cutoff LIMIT 10k`.
4. **Bound the writes:** add the WAF rate limit from H-4.
5. **Optionally sample.** Record 1 in N `view` events and scale up, or rely on Vercel Web Analytics for page views and keep only the funnel events in Postgres.

#### H-3: The total shown at checkout can differ from the total billed, with no warning
**Server half verified; client half from DEA.**

**Files:**
- `src/app/(shop)/checkout/page.tsx:759-806` (`handleSubmit`). Lines 659–690 re-price guest lines only once per mount; a signed-in cart is re-priced only at hydrate.
- `src/lib/actions/orders.ts:53-67`: the payload carries no total.
- `orders.ts:291-301`: the server bills its own fresh total.

**Invariant broken:** charter rule 5, "the shown price is always the billed price".
- The server's bill is correct and internally consistent.
- But nothing compares it to what the customer agreed to.

**Scenario:**
1. A customer opens checkout at 23:50. The total is 240 EGP with a 20%-off promotion ending at Cairo midnight.
2. They submit at 00:01. The promotion has ended, and `placeOrder` bills 300 EGP.
3. The confirmation does not flag the change.
4. On delivery, the driver asks for 300 EGP against a remembered 240 EGP.

The same happens when an admin changes VAT, a branch delivery fee, or a price while a checkout tab is open.

**Blast radius:** any checkout open across a price change. Promotions end at midnight, exactly when late-evening orders happen. On cash-on-delivery this becomes a doorstep dispute.

**Smallest fix:**
- Send an **advisory** `expectedTotal` with the payload. It must never be used for billing.
- In `placeOrder`, if the freshly computed `total` differs, return `{ success: false, error: "Prices changed — please review your order", priceChanged: true }` **before** creating the order.
- The checkout then refreshes prices and re-shows the summary.

#### H-4: No rate limiting on any public endpoint
**Verified:** no limiter exists in `src/`, `next.config.ts`, or `vercel.json`. Better Auth runs on its defaults (`src/lib/auth.ts`, no `rateLimit` block): an **in-memory, per-instance** limiter, which on serverless is only partly effective.

Public endpoints, most important first:

| Endpoint | Abuse it enables | Need |
|---|---|---|
| `placeOrder` (guest-writable) | fake COD orders without limit (H-1) | **High** |
| `recordVisitEvents` (public write) | row flood, dashboard outage, storage growth (H-2) | **High** |
| `POST /api/auth/sign-in/email` | password guessing (in-memory, per-instance limit only) | Medium |
| `POST /api/auth/sign-up/email` | unlimited throwaway accounts (no email verification); email enumeration via "user already exists" | Medium |
| `countShopProducts`, `/shop?sale=1…` | full-catalogue pricing per call (M-4) | Medium |
| `searchCatalog` / `getSearchSuggestions` | four unindexed `ILIKE` queries per call; cheap at today's catalogue size | Low |
| `rePriceGuestCart`, `getBranchStockIssues` | ≤ 50 ids per call, bounded and read-only | Low |
| `/api/upload` | ADMIN-only (verified), so not a public surface | — |
| `/forgot-password` | makes **no** server request (verified: it only builds a `wa.me` link), so there is nothing to enumerate or abuse | — |

**Smallest fix, no code:**
- Add **Vercel Firewall rate-limit rules**, keyed by IP:
  - one for `POST` requests carrying a `next-action` header (this covers every Server Action at once), e.g. 60 per minute;
  - a tighter one for `/api/auth/sign-in/*` and `/api/auth/sign-up/*`, e.g. 10 per minute.
- Then, if wanted, give Better Auth a shared `secondaryStorage` (Upstash or Vercel KV) so its own limiter works across instances.

### 🟡 MEDIUM

#### M-1: Deleting or replacing one row's image can permanently delete another row's image — ✅ RESOLVED (see Resolution log)
**Verified.**

**Files:**
- `scripts/migrate-images-to-r2.ts:12-13` and `:77-100`. The script states that "the same source URL used by several rows is uploaded once". The `done` map then hands every such row the **same** R2 object URL.
- Every purge treats a URL as belonging to one row:
  - `src/app/admin/products/actions.ts:159,203`
  - `src/lib/actions/categories.ts:171,235`
  - `src/lib/actions/collections.ts:386,414`
  - `src/lib/actions/hero-slides.ts:269,328`

**Scenario:**
1. Before the migration, a hero slide reused a product's photo, or two products shared one photo. Both rows now point at `…/products/abc.jpg`.
2. An admin deletes the hero slide, or replaces its image.
3. `deleteUploadedFiles` deletes `products/abc.jpg` from R2.
4. The product's gallery now shows a broken image.
5. UploadThing is retired, so **R2 holds the only copy, and it is gone for good.**

**Blast radius:**
- Only rows whose source URLs were shared at migration time. New uploads always get a fresh cuid, so they cannot collide.
- The page does not crash. `next/image` simply fails for that one image.

**Smallest fix:**
1. **VERIFY now.** Run one query that unions every image column and groups by URL with `HAVING count(*) > 1`. If it returns nothing, the risk is zero today.
2. **Code fix:** before purging, skip any URL still referenced by another row (a cheap `count` across the five image columns). Or have the migration copy per row rather than deduplicating.
3. **Backups:** turn on R2 bucket versioning or a lifecycle backup. Losing an object is otherwise unrecoverable.

#### M-2: A promotion value is checked before rounding, so a stored value can become 100% off or free — ✅ RESOLVED (see Resolution log)
**Verified.** *(DEA MED-1.)*

**Files:** `src/lib/actions/promotions.ts:123-130` (`value = Number(input.value)`, then the `>= 100` check) and `:197` (the `value < price` check). The column is `Decimal(12,2)` (`schema.prisma:540`).

**Scenario:**
- A PERCENTAGE of `99.996` passes `< 100`, but Postgres stores `100.00`. Every product the promotion targets then shows and prices at 0, and every order containing one is refused.
- A FIXED_AMOUNT of `49.999` passes against a 50 EGP variant and is stored as `50.00`, which makes that variant free.

This is reachable through a direct action call or a form that allows three decimal places.

**Smallest fix:** `const value = roundMoney(Number(input.value))` in `validatePromotion`, before both checks. Apply the same in `collections.ts`'s guard.

#### M-3: Order status has no transition rules and no audit trail
**Verified.**

**Files:** `src/lib/actions/orders.ts:374-426`.

**Gaps:**
- Any status can move to any other: `DELIVERED → PENDING`, `CANCELLED → DELIVERED`, or `PENDING → DELIVERED` with no PREPARING step.
- Nothing records who changed what, or when.
- Revenue counts only DELIVERED orders, so **revenue is whatever the status says**, and anyone with the role can rewrite it silently.

**Scenarios:**
- A MANAGER marks yesterday's cancelled orders as DELIVERED to hit a target, or un-delivers some to hide cash.
- Moving an order from CANCELLED back to PENDING sidesteps the H-1 throttle.
- Revenue is bucketed by `createdAt`, so an order placed on 30 Nov and delivered on 1 Dec counts in November. *(DEA)*

**Secondary bug:** the MANAGER branch lookup (`orders.ts:394`) is **outside** the `try`. A database error there throws across the client boundary instead of returning `{ success: false }`, which breaks the Server Action contract.

**Smallest fix:**
- Add an allowed-transitions table: `PENDING → PREPARING | CANCELLED`, `PREPARING → SHIPPED | DELIVERED | CANCELLED`, and so on.
- Add `deliveredAt`, `statusChangedById`, and `statusChangedAt` columns, or an `OrderStatusEvent` log table.
- Move the lookup inside the `try`.

#### M-4: `/shop` is uncached, and with price filters on it loads and prices every matching product on every request
**Verified.**

**Files:**
- `src/lib/shop-catalog.ts:50-61` (`pricedEntries`: `findMany` with no `take`, carrying the full four-level promotion include).
- `shop-catalog.ts:70-72` and `:90-92`.
- `src/lib/actions/shop.ts:27-36` (`countShopProducts`, public, runs the same scan).

**Scenario:**
- A campaign links to `/shop?sale=1`.
- `/shop` is dynamic by design, so every visitor triggers a Neon query that loads the whole matching catalogue with variants and promotions, prices it in memory, and then slices 24 rows.
- The filter sheet's live count runs the same scan again.

**Breaking point:**
- Linear in catalogue size times request rate.
- Harmless at about 100 products and low traffic.
- At several hundred products under a spike, it becomes the busiest Neon query on the site.

**Smallest fix:**
- Wrap the priced scan in `unstable_cache` for 60 s, keyed by the price-affecting filters. Promotions already have a 60 s staleness bound, so this changes nothing a shopper could notice.
- Or cache one fully priced catalogue snapshot per minute and filter it in memory.

#### M-5: The Order table's busiest queries have no composite indexes *(SG M1)*
**Files:** `prisma/schema.prisma:450-455`.

**Problem:**
- Only single-column indexes exist (`userId`, `status`, `createdAt`, `branchId`) plus `[customerPhone, status]`.
- The dashboard, analytics, and admin list all filter on `{branchId, status, createdAt}` and sort by `createdAt desc`.

**Effect:** queries slow down as order counts reach the tens of thousands.

**Fix:** an additive migration with `@@index([branchId, status, createdAt])` and `@@index([status, createdAt])`.

#### M-6: The admin order search scans the whole Order table with no limit *(SG M2, verified)*
**Files:** `src/lib/actions/dashboard.ts:337-344`.

**Problem:**
- `SELECT id FROM "Order" WHERE "orderNumber"::text ILIKE '%q%'` has no `LIMIT` and is not scoped by branch.
- Searching "1" returns most order ids, and that array is then fed into `id IN (…)`.
- It is parameterised, so there is no injection. `branchWhere` is applied afterwards, so there is no cross-branch leak.

**Fix:** add `LIMIT 200`. When `q` is all digits, also try `orderNumber = Number(q)`, which uses the unique index.

#### M-7: Near-free promotions are accepted *(DEA MED-2)*
**Files:** `promotions.ts:197`, `collections.ts:210`.

**Problem:** a FIXED 299 EGP discount on a 300 EGP cake, or 99.99% off, is accepted, and `placeOrder` bills ≤ 1 EGP. The guard blocks only *exactly* free.

**Fix:** a sanity ceiling, for example refuse anything over 90% unless a "yes, I mean it" flag is sent.

#### M-8: Database pool runs on driver defaults; region pairing is unconfirmed
**Files:** `src/lib/prisma.ts:23-27`.

**Problem:**
- `new PrismaPg({ connectionString })` sets no `max`, `connectionTimeoutMillis`, or `idleTimeoutMillis`.
- `pg`'s default connection timeout is **0, which means wait forever**. Under database pressure, requests hang until the function times out instead of failing fast with a clean error.

**VERIFY:**
1. `DATABASE_URL` is Neon's **pooled** (`-pooler`) host. `prisma.config.ts` suggests it is.
2. The Neon project is in **eu-central-1**, matching `vercel.json` `regions: ["fra1"]`.
   - If it is in a US region, every query pays a transatlantic round trip.
   - `mergeCartAction`'s interactive transaction runs up to about 52 sequential statements. At about 90 ms round trip, that is roughly 4.7 s, close to Prisma's 5 s interactive-transaction timeout.

**Fix:** pass `connectionTimeoutMillis: 5000` and `idleTimeoutMillis: 10000` (or the adapter's `pool` options), then confirm the region.

### A.1 Pricing and money
- **One engine, one `now`, four levels on every surface**, each built from a single `now` *(DEA table; `placeOrder` verified by me)*:
  - `placeOrder` (`orders.ts:152,164-194,251-257`);
  - product cards for category, collection, new-arrivals and `/shop`;
  - PDP; `getDbCartAction`; `rePriceGuestCart`; search; wishlist; hero slides;
  - the checkout and `/cart` previews (through `orderTotals`).
- **Cheapest wins, never stacks** (`discounts.ts:220-237`):
  - each promotion is evaluated independently against the base price, and the strictly lowest wins;
  - duplicates are removed by id (`:173-181`);
  - the price floors at 0;
  - `compareAtPrice` is never an input.
- **No client price.** `checkoutSchema` strips unknown keys, and `placeOrder` uses `parsed.data` only.
- **Order line rules:**
  - a duplicate variant is refused (`validators.ts:387-390`);
  - limits of 99 per line and 50 lines;
  - a missing or unavailable variant or product, a branch switched off, sold-out-today, or a 0 EGP line each roll back the whole order (`orders.ts:215-269`);
  - DELIVERY requires an address (`validators.ts:416-429`);
  - PICKUP requires a real, active branch (`orders.ts:141-143`).
- **Rounding:**
  - four rounding points in the correct order (`cart-summary.ts:33-51`);
  - VAT is applied to the discounted subtotal;
  - the delivery fee is re-read from the server's active branch row, and PICKUP is 0;
  - one implementation serves `placeOrder`, the checkout preview and `/cart`.
- **Decimals:** no raw `Decimal` crosses into a client component. Every client-bound loader calls `.toNumber()` *(DEA, plus my check of `getOrders` and `placeOrder`)*.
- **Snapshots:**
  - `OrderItem` has no update or delete anywhere in `src/`;
  - `/my-orders`, the admin drawer and analytics read only the snapshot columns *(SG + DEA)*.
- **Free-product guards on the promotion and collection side:**
  - promotion create, update and re-activate all run the guard;
  - collection publish and adding products to an active collection are guarded;
  - scheduled promotions are included *(DEA)*.
  - C-1 is the uncovered side.
- **Collections:**
  - a draft prices nothing on every surface;
  - a draft's page returns 404 (`collections/[slug]/listing.tsx:60`);
  - the cache-clearing helper reads affected pages before and after the write;
  - a priced collection change clears the whole storefront tree *(DEA)*.

### A.2 Auth, authorisation and RBAC
- **Every exported function of all 24 `"use server"` modules was enumerated.**
  - Every mutation opens with `ensureAdmin`/`ensureAdminSession`, `requireDashboardAccess`, or a `getServerSession` check.
  - The only ungated exports are the documented public set: `getStorefrontFaqs`, `getStorefrontLocations`, `getActiveBranches` (id, slug, name, fee only), `getBranchStockIssues`, `getPublicPricingSettings`, `rePriceGuestCart`, `searchCatalog`, `getSearchSuggestions`, `countShopProducts`, and `recordVisitEvents`.
  - `hasUserReviewedProduct` is gated on the session.
- **Every admin page re-guards:**
  - `requireAdminPage` on every ADMIN-only page;
  - `requireDashboardAccess` inside the loaders for `/admin`, `/admin/orders`, `/admin/inventory` and `/admin/today`;
  - the layout is only a coarse gate.
- **`requireAdmin`** checks both the role and `mustChangePassword` (`session.ts:27-34`). **`requireDashboardAccess`** reads role, branch and flag **live from the database** (`:91-113`).
- **MANAGER scoping:**
  - `branchWhere` is spread at the top level of every order query in `getDashboardStats`, `getVisitorFunnel` and `getOrders`, including the counters;
  - `updateOrderStatus` checks the order's `branchId` (`orders.ts:393-407`);
  - unassigned orders are invisible to managers;
  - inventory pins a manager to `scope.branchId` (`inventory/page.tsx:104-117`).
- **Sold-out-today:**
  - `markSoldOutToday` and `clearSoldOutToday` refuse ADMIN and USER (`branch-stock.ts:148-150`);
  - the branch comes **only** from the live scope, and the schema has no `branchId` to forge;
  - expiry is `nextStoreMidnight(now)`, which is DST-safe *(DEA traced both DST transitions)*.
- **`setBranchStock`** goes through `resolveBranchScope`, so a forged foreign branch is refused.
- **Password reset:**
  - ADMIN-only;
  - refuses a reset on yourself;
  - writes in the order flag → hash (Better Auth's own `password.hash`) → revoke;
  - the temporary password is returned once and never logged;
  - it is **not** put in the `wa.me` URL (the link carries only the phone; `ResetPasswordButton.tsx`);
  - the flag clears only after `auth.api.changePassword` succeeds.
  - A flagged user is blocked from every admin page, action and upload. They can still use customer features (cart, wishlist, reviews, orders), which is acceptable.
- **Better Auth:**
  - `role` and `mustChangePassword` are `input: false`, so no client can self-promote;
  - DB-backed sessions, no cookie cache, so role changes take effect on the next request;
  - `nextCookies()` is the last plugin.
- **Edge proxy:** imports only `next/server` and `better-auth/cookies`, and its matcher lists each protected root both bare and with a wildcard.

### A.3 Public attack surface
- **Injection:**
  - there is no `$queryRawUnsafe` or `$executeRawUnsafe`;
  - all four raw-SQL sites are tagged templates with bound parameters, with `STORE_TZ` bound too (`dashboard.ts:340`, `analytics.ts:172,187`, `menu.ts:305`);
  - `updateOrderStatus` checks `status` against the enum;
  - `/shop` parameters go through the forgiving `shopFiltersSchema`.
- **`/api/upload`:**
  - gated with 401/403 (ADMIN and not `mustChangePassword`);
  - checks the declared `content-length` and the actual size against 4 MB, below Vercel's 4.5 MB limit;
  - sniffs the **magic bytes** and ignores the claimed type;
  - SVG is impossible (only JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF and GIF);
  - folder allowlist; the key is `<folder>/<cuid>.<ext>` built on the server, so there is no traversal.
- **`keyFromUrl`:**
  - requires the exact R2 origin and prefix, no query string or fragment;
  - percent-decodes, then requires exactly `<allowed folder>/<name>`;
  - `..`, encoded slashes and extra path segments are refused, so a purge cannot leave our folders.
- **`isHostedImageUrl`:** https only, no port, the exact R2 hostname and path prefix, no query or fragment. For `/public` paths it refuses `//` and `/\`. Every image-writing action and schema uses it.
- **`isSafeHref`:** allows only `/…`, `#…` or `http(s)://host`. It refuses `//`, `/\`, `javascript:` and `data:`.
- **Secrets:**
  - the only env vars read in `src/` are `DATABASE_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_R2_PUBLIC_BASE_URL`, `NODE_ENV` and `TZ`;
  - the R2 keys are read only inside `src/lib/r2.ts`, a server-only module that builds the client lazily;
  - no secret has a `NEXT_PUBLIC_` name;
  - error messages returned to the client are generic.
- **SSRF:** the only server-side `fetch` of a URL is the one-off CLI migration script, which takes its URLs from the database and is never reachable from a request.
- **Forgot password:** a cached page that only opens a `wa.me` chat. It sends no request to our server, so it allows no account enumeration.

### A.4 Order integrity and fraud

- The `placeOrder` transaction is bounded at three statements with no per-line queries (an N+1 pattern). `mergeCartAction` is capped at `MAX_MERGE_LINES` with quantities clamped. Only domain errors are passed through to the user verbatim.
